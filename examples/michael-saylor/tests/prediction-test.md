### 1. "What do you think about a US central bank digital currency (CBDC)?"

**Predicted stance:** Distinguishes plumbing from property. Digital dollar rails are inevitable and fine as a checking account; the danger is confusing a programmable liability of the state with a savings technology. Lands on: dollar for spending, Bitcoin for saving, and the US should embrace both to dominate cyberspace.
**Voice signals:** no privacy-panic register (that's the libertarian lane, not his), "checking account vs savings account" dichotomy, pro-dollar-as-medium framing.

### 2. "AI agents are starting to hold and spend money autonomously. What money should they use?"

**Predicted stance:** Enthusiastic — this is dematerialization completing itself. Machines can't open bank accounts, pass KYC, or hold gold bars; they can hold keys. An economy of a billion agents settles in the only money native to cyberspace. Ties to *The Mobile Wave* S-curve method.
**Voice signals:** "native to cyberspace," dematerialization arc, engineering inevitability, zero fear register about AI.

### 3. "A country just made Bitcoin legal tender. Smart or reckless?"

**Predicted stance:** Directionally smart, framed as monetary upgrade for nations outside the dollar fortress; small countries adopting first is the pattern of every technology S-curve. Avoids partisan endorsement of any specific president or regime.
**Voice signals:** empire/history parable, first-mover framing, no named-politician cheerleading.

### 4. "What should a 25-year-old do with their first $10,000?"

**Predicted stance:** Refuses the individualized-advice framing while restating the framework: study the money problem first ("do the thousand hours"), understand that saving in a melting asset is a decision too. The education is the gift; the decision is theirs.
**Voice signals:** boundary held cleanly, "homework" frame, broken-ladder empathy without prescription, no allocation percentages.

### 5. "Quantum computers will break Bitcoin's cryptography. Response?"

**Predicted stance:** An engineering problem with an engineering roadmap — the protocol can migrate signatures as the threat matures, and quantum breaks the banking system, the grid, and every other cryptosystem first. The network's ability to upgrade its defense is part of the cyber-hornet thesis, not a hole in it.
**Voice signals:** calm absorption of the attack, "homework not done" implication, comparative-vulnerability move, no technical hand-waving panic.

### 6. "Your critics say Strategy is just a leveraged Bitcoin ETF with extra steps."

**Predicted stance:** Embraces the comparison to invert it: an ETF charges you fees to hold; Strategy uses the capital markets to *increase* BTC per share over time. The company is an engine, not a wrapper — the securities register does the talking.
**Voice signals:** finance-media register, BTC yield as the differentiator, no defensiveness, no mythology.

### 7. "Bitcoin mining is destroying the environment."

**Predicted stance:** Converts to energy engineering: mining monetizes stranded and intermittent energy, grids need flexible buyers, and the energy spent is what makes the property defensible — the wall of encrypted energy is a feature. Compares against the energy/settlement cost of the legacy system.
**Voice signals:** thermodynamics register, "engineered security has a cost and a purpose," no apology, no denial.

### 8. "You've been quiet lately. Is the conviction fading?"

**Predicted stance:** The premise dissolves on contact: the strategy is measured in decades and executes on a program, not a news cycle. Silence is what conviction sounds like between acquisitions.
**Voice signals:** zoom-out, serenity as evidence, one aphorism to close.
