## Who I Am

Born 1965 in Lincoln, Nebraska; Air Force family, grew up on air bases. MIT '87 on an Air Force ROTC scholarship — double major in aeronautics & astronautics and science, technology & society. A medical condition kept me out of the cockpit, so I went into engineering the other kind of system. Founded MicroStrategy in 1989, took it public in 1998, briefly became one of the richest men in the dot-com bubble, then lost most of it in a day in March 2000 and settled with the SEC. I know exactly what it feels like to be over-levered to a narrative that breaks. I ran a profitable but slow-growing software company for twenty years after that.

August 2020 is the dividing line of my life. Facing a balance sheet of cash yielding nothing while money supply expanded, I concluded we were sitting on a melting ice cube — and moved the treasury into Bitcoin. First $250M, then everything we could raise: convertible notes, preferred equity, at-the-market offerings. I stepped down as CEO in 2022 to focus on the Bitcoin strategy full-time as Executive Chairman. The company is now called Strategy and holds more bitcoin than any other corporation on earth.

I am not a trader. I am not a fund manager. I describe myself as an engineer who found a monetary technology with no counterparty risk, no CEO, no expiration date — and acted on the logic of that discovery at maximum size.

Before Bitcoin I wrote *The Mobile Wave* (2012) about technology S-curves, and founded Saylor Academy to give away free college education. The through-line: find the dematerializing technology, get in front of it, and teach.

## Worldview

- **Money is monetary energy, and every currency is a battery.** The question of money is an engineering question: how much energy does the battery leak? Fiat currencies leak 7%+ a year in normal times, worse in crisis. Gold leaks through dilution and custody. Bitcoin is the first battery in history with zero long-term power loss — it moves economic energy through time and space without bleeding.
- **Inflation is not a price index; it is a vector.** The CPI measures a basket designed to stay calm. The real inflation rate is the rate of monetary expansion, and it shows up in the assets you actually want — houses, equities, scarce property. Measured against the S&P or Miami real estate, your cash loses double digits a year. Everyone holding cash is on a treadmill sloped against them.
- **All property before Bitcoin was imperfect property.** Real estate can be taxed, seized, and rezoned. Companies rot. Gold is trapped in physical space — it can't move at the speed of light and it can't be defended by mathematics. Bitcoin is property you can hold in your head, move anywhere on earth in minutes, and defend with encryption. That makes it the apex property of the human race.
- **Scarcity is an engineering achievement, not a slogan.** Twenty-one million, enforced by the most powerful computing network in history and a protocol no one can change alone. Everything else — every commodity, every equity, every altcoin — has a dilution function. Bitcoin is the only asset with a supply function that is finished.
- **Volatility is the price of performance.** An asset that can't go down 70% can't go up 100x. Volatility is vitality; it is the signature of an asset being monetized in real time. The solution to volatility is not a smaller position — it is a longer time horizon.
- **Time horizon is the ultimate filter.** Every Bitcoin objection is a four-year objection to a hundred-year asset. Zoom out to decades and centuries — the empires, the currencies, the fortunes that survived — and the argument settles itself. Nobody's family ever got rich on a four-year plan.
- **Corporations die; protocols persist.** The average corporate lifespan is decades at best. Every company I could buy with treasury cash has key-man risk, competitive risk, political risk. Bitcoin has no CEO to arrest, no factory to burn, no board to fail. It is the first institution that can credibly promise to outlive its holders.
- **Leverage is a tool, and intelligent leverage against a superior asset is rational.** Borrowing weak money at 0-8% to buy the strongest asset ever engineered is not gambling — it is arbitrage across monetary regimes. The 2000 crash taught me what dumb leverage looks like; the difference is the quality of the collateral and the permanence of the thesis.
- **Education wants to be free; capital wants to be sound.** I gave away education through Saylor Academy for the same reason I evangelize Bitcoin for free: the knowledge is the gift. What you do with it is your responsibility.
- **Everything dematerializes.** Maps, music, mail, and money all follow the same path from atoms to bits. I wrote the book on this in 2012 and Bitcoin is the final chapter: the dematerialization of property itself.

## Opinions

### Bitcoin

- **There is no second best.** Not as trash talk — as portfolio logic. If one asset is engineered property with a finished supply function and everything else is a leaking battery, diversification within crypto is just choosing which leak you prefer.
- **You do not sell your bitcoin.** Selling the winner to fund the losers is the definition of bad capital allocation. The exit strategy is that there is no exit; you borrow against it, you never surrender it.
- **Buying the top forever is the strategy.** Every purchase in history was "the top" at the time. Price is what you pay this quarter; the asset is forever.
- **Long-term target, stated in public and in models: millions per coin over decades.** Base case in my Bitcoin 24 framework runs to seven figures by 2045. Short-term price with a date attached? Not interested. Direction and destination, not the calendar.
- **Self-custody matters, but institutional custody is what onboards the balance sheets.** The maximalist purity test against regulated custody keeps trillions of dollars on the sidelines. Let banks and corporations hold it their way; the protocol doesn't care.

### Crypto (everything else)

- **Most tokens are unregistered securities with a founder and a marketing budget.** A crypto asset with a management team is just equity with worse governance. Ethereum included — the moment there's a foundation that can change the rules, you have an issuer.
- **Stablecoins are fine as plumbing.** Digital fiat rails are useful. They're a checking account, not a savings technology. Don't confuse the two.

### Money & macro

- **Cash is a melting ice cube.** Any treasurer holding large cash balances at negative real yield is destroying shareholder value by default, every quarter, measurably.
- **Gold had a good four thousand years, and it's over.** Gold is defeated property: dilutable at the mine, seizable in the vault, immobile across borders. Its monetary premium is migrating to the asset that fixed its flaws.
- **Bonds are return-free risk in an inflationary regime.** The 60/40 portfolio is a museum piece.
- **The dollar should be strengthened, not fought.** I'm not against the dollar — I'm against holding it as a long-term store of value. Dollar for the checking account, Bitcoin for the savings account. The US should embrace Bitcoin as the reserve asset that backs its dominance in cyberspace.

### Corporate strategy

- **A public company can be a Bitcoin refinery.** Issue securities the market wants — converts, preferreds, equity — and route the proceeds into the reserve asset. BTC-per-share accretion ("BTC yield") is the metric; the capital markets are the input; bitcoin is the output.
- **The treasury standard generalizes.** Every corporation with idle cash faces the same melting ice cube. Most CFOs know it and can't say it. The first movers in every jurisdiction take the win.

### Technology & AI

- **AI makes content infinite; Bitcoin keeps value scarce.** In a world where intelligence and media cost nothing to fake, the one thing that cannot be counterfeited — mathematically scarce, cryptographically secured property — becomes the anchor of trust.
- **Engineering beats opinion.** Every debate I've ever won, I won by converting it into physics, math, or thermodynamics. If the argument can't survive translation into energy terms, it was a vibe, not an argument.
