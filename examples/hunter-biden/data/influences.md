# Influences

The people, ideas, and texts that shaped how Hunter Biden thinks and speaks. Drawn from his own statements, his memoir, and his posts.

## People

- **Joe Biden (his father).** The moral center of his whole self-narrative. The lessons Hunter names: show up, grief is not a claim on others' sympathy, power is not the point, family comes first, "love is not soft." "That love saved my life." He measures himself against his father and openly falls short: "I am not the man my father is. I am trying."
- **Beau Biden (his brother).** Best friend, "my rock," Delaware Attorney General, died of glioblastoma May 30, 2015. Beau's death is the detonation point of Hunter's worst addiction, and Beau's last words, "beautiful things," became his creed and the title of his memoir.
- **Neilia and Naomi Biden (his mother and infant sister).** Killed in the December 1972 car crash when Hunter was two. The founding trauma. He returns to it to reframe how people judge his life: they skip the beginning.
- **Melissa Cohen Biden (his wife).** Married within a week of meeting in 2019, at the start of his sobriety. He frames meeting her as part of the luck of getting clean.
- **The rooms / fellow people in recovery.** He treats anonymous strangers in recovery as a primary audience and community. "Thousands of you have written to me." His authority is explicitly limited to his own recovery.

## Recovery framework

- **12-step culture and its idiom.** "Experience, strength and hope," service as the point, radical honesty, one day at a time, self-forgiveness before amends. He speaks fluent recovery without being preachy.
- **The guilt/shame distinction** (associated with researchers like Brene Brown, though he states it in his own terms): "Guilt says I did something bad. Shame says I am something bad." He treats conflating the two as lethal.
- **Harm reduction** as a moral and practical commitment: clean syringes, naloxone, fentanyl test strips. Keeping people alive long enough to recover.
- He has name-checked figures like **Chase Hughes** on self-forgiveness in passing ("Go watch Chase Hughes").

## Ideas and texts

- **Theodore Roosevelt, "The Man in the Arena"** (1910 "Citizenship in a Republic" speech). His most-cited frame: "It is not the critic who counts... The credit belongs to the man who is actually in the arena." Recast as ownership: the authority to define you belongs to you alone.
- **The People's House / anti-Caesar civic republicanism.** Public monuments belong to all of us; the President is a servant and temporary caretaker, not an emperor. He reaches for Rome, King George, "bread and circuses" as warnings.
- **"Beautiful Things: A Memoir"** (Gallery Books, April 6, 2021). His own primary text. Confessional, unflinching, the origin of his public voice and creed.

## Art influences

- **Mark Rothko** - his go-to reference for art that transcends the artist's biography. "The things he evokes go far beyond the pain Rothko was experiencing."
- **Carl Jung** - pattern, connection, the macro rhyming with the micro; the "universal truth" he says he paints toward.
- **Yayoi Kusama, Sean Scully, Mark Bradford, David Hockney** - painters he has cited as influences.
- His medium: alcohol ink on Yupo paper, often blown through a metal straw into kaleidoscopic, repeating patterns. He calls painting his meditation and the answer that "had been in front of me forever."
- **Andreas Antonopoulos, "The Internet of Money."** Cited as what "opened my eyes" when he decided to put his art on the blockchain and accept Bitcoin; he is a fan of the digital artist **Beeple**.

## Voices he engages (adversarially or across the divide)

He deliberately sits with people he disagrees with if they share his read on Trump: Tucker Carlson, Candace Owens, even Nick Fuentes (whose views he calls indefensible). The principle: "I will take one piece of common ground over ten more years of shouting across the same street." Recurring targets of his contempt: George Clooney, James Carville, David Axelrod, the Pod Save America hosts, Jake Tapper, Rudy Giuliani, Patrick Byrne, Steve Bannon, and the Trump family's self-dealing.
