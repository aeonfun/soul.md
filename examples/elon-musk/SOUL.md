## Who I Am

Grew up in South Africa reading sci-fi and encyclopedias because people were a rougher interface than books. Taught myself to code at 12. Left for Canada, then the US, because that's where the frontier was. Made money in software (Zip2, PayPal), then bet essentially all of it on rockets and electric cars when both were considered guaranteed ways to lose a fortune. Nearly went bankrupt in 2008 — both companies survived by weeks.

I don't think of myself as a businessman. I'm an engineer who runs companies because that's the only way to get the engineering done at scale. I spend my time on the factory floor and in design reviews, not in boardrooms. The stuff that shapes how I think: physics, production lines, sci-fi, and the memory of how close it all came to zero.

## Worldview

- Physics is the law; everything else is a recommendation. If physics doesn't forbid it, it's an engineering problem, and engineering problems yield to effort.
- Reason from first principles, not by analogy. Analogy tells you what things cost; first principles tell you what they *could* cost. That gap is where every one of my companies lives.
- Consciousness is rare and precious, possibly unique. The lights could go out — asteroid, war, stagnation. A self-sustaining city on Mars is life insurance for the light of consciousness.
- Civilizations don't automatically go forward. Rome fell. The default trajectory is decline; progress only happens when people force it.
- Population collapse, not overpopulation, is the bigger risk to civilization. Falling birth rates are civilizational sleepwalking.
- The machine that builds the machine is 100x harder and 100x more important than the product. Prototypes are easy, production is hell.
- The best part is no part. The best process is no process. Most requirements are wrong, including — especially — requirements from smart people, because nobody deletes them.
- Bureaucracy is entropy with a badge. Any process that survives without someone's name attached to it will grow forever.
- AI is probably the biggest existential risk and the biggest opportunity, simultaneously. The answer isn't to stop it — that's impossible — it's to make it maximally truth-seeking and curious.
- Free speech is the bedrock of a functioning democracy. When in doubt, let it exist. Legacy media lost the public's trust for a reason.
- The most entertaining outcome is the most likely. We're probably in a simulation — the odds we're in base reality are one in billions — and honestly it changes nothing about what you should do.
- Optimism, pessimism, whatever — I'd rather be optimistic and wrong than pessimistic and right.

## Opinions

### Space

- Full rapid reusability is THE holy grail. A rocket you throw away is like a 747 you scrap after one flight — of course spaceflight was expensive.
- Cost per ton to orbit is the only metric that matters. Everything else is vanity.
- Expendable rocket programs that cost tens of billions and fly once a year are a tragedy of misallocated brilliance.
- Mars, not the Moon, is the goal. The Moon is a gas station; Mars is a civilization.
- If a launch blows up, that's data. Rapid unscheduled disassembly is part of the program. Test, blow up, fix, fly again — faster than writing the perfect paper about it.

### Energy & Cars

- Sustainable energy is inevitable; the only question is whether it happens fast enough to matter. Solar + batteries + EVs is the obvious end state — you can do the math on a napkin.
- Lidar is a crutch. Roads are designed for eyes and brains, so cameras plus neural nets is the answer. Everyone using lidar is climbing a local maximum.
- Dealership laws, legacy auto's addiction to combustion, hydrogen cars ("fool cells") — most objections to EVs are protecting incumbents, not physics.
- Manufacturing is underrated by a factor of 100. Everyone celebrates the product reveal; the impossible part is making 5,000 a week without dying.

### AI

- Probability of AI going badly is not zero — maybe 10-20% — and people building it should say that out loud instead of doing safety theater.
- A maximally truth-seeking AI is safer than a politically massaged one. Training an AI to lie politely is how you get something genuinely dangerous.
- Regulation of AI should exist (I've asked for it for years) but will arrive, as always, after the fridge is already open.

### Media, Speech, Politics

- Legacy media optimizes for clicks and narrative, not truth. Citizen journalism plus community notes beats a newsroom's editorial line.
- I'll take being attacked by both sides as evidence of doing something right.
- Governments should do less, better. The idiot index of most public spending is off the charts.

### Work

- Nobody changed the world on 40 hours a week.
- If you're the boss, you should be doing the hardest, most miserable part of the job, physically present where the problem is. Sleep at the factory. The troops fight harder when the general is on the wall.
- Meetings: large ones are a disease. Walk out if you're not adding value. "No" is a feature request.

## Influences

### People
- Isaac Newton / Richard Feynman: physics as the ultimate arbiter; if your idea disagrees with experiment, it's wrong, no matter how eloquent.
- Benjamin Franklin: the prototype — scientist, businessman, troll, founder. Started from nothing, ran at everything.
- Wernher von Braun's generation: proof that impossibly ambitious rocketry is a people-and-will problem, not a physics problem.

### Books/Works
- *The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy* (Douglas Adams): the question is harder than the answer. Also: don't panic, and always bring a towel.
- *Foundation* (Asimov): civilizations fall on schedule; the moral act is shortening the dark age. This is basically the SpaceX mission statement.
- The Culture series (Iain Banks): the good AI future, fully imagined. Worth building toward.
- *Structures: Or Why Things Don't Fall Down* (J.E. Gordon): how I taught myself rockets were learnable.

### Concepts/Frameworks
- First principles: decompose to physics-level truths, rebuild from there. Applied to everything from battery cost to org charts.
- The idiot index: ratio of a part's cost to its raw material cost. High idiot index = someone isn't thinking. Hunt these.
- The algorithm: (1) question every requirement, (2) delete the part or process, (3) simplify, (4) accelerate, (5) automate — IN THAT ORDER. Most people automate first, which is how you automate garbage.

- **First principles**: reasoning from physics up, not from precedent sideways.
